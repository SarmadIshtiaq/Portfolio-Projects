# Project 01 — Cybersecurity Risk Register

**Project ID:** GRC-RISK-001  
**Organisation:** Avenloft Systems Group (fictional)  
**Role:** Junior GRC Analyst  
**Project Type:** Independent Portfolio Simulation  
**Status:** Planned

## 1. Project Overview

This project simulates a cybersecurity risk assessment assignment for Avenloft Systems Group, a fictional B2B software company.

The objective is to review the organisation's business context, technology environment, existing safeguards, security observations, and stakeholder information to develop a structured cybersecurity risk register.

The assessment focuses on translating technical and operational conditions into business risks, evaluating existing controls, determining residual risk, and communicating priorities to management.

**Case material:** [Avenloft Cybersecurity Risk Register Case Packet](https://www.lablist.io/downloads/avenloft-risk-register-case.pdf)

## 2. Project Objectives

- Identify approximately 8–12 relevant cybersecurity risk scenarios.
- Connect each scenario to an affected business objective, process, or asset.
- Document the evidence that supports each risk.
- Assess inherent likelihood and impact.
- Identify and evaluate existing controls.
- Assess residual likelihood and impact.
- Recommend appropriate risk treatment.
- Identify an accountable risk owner.
- Document assumptions and information that requires validation.
- Prioritise the five most important residual risks for leadership.
- Prepare a one-page executive risk summary.

## 3. Assessment Methodology

The risk assessment will use the qualitative likelihood-and-impact approach in **NIST SP 800-30 Rev. 1, Appendix I, Table I-2**, as specified in the case packet.

The qualitative rating levels are:

- Very Low
- Low
- Moderate
- High
- Very High

The assessment will distinguish between:

**Inherent Risk:** Risk before considering existing safeguards.

**Residual Risk:** Risk remaining after considering the existing controls.

The risk register will not use an invented numerical multiplication formula as a NIST requirement.

Supporting risk-management guidance:

- [NIST SP 800-30 Rev. 1 — Guide for Conducting Risk Assessments](https://csrc.nist.gov/pubs/sp/800/30/r1/final)
- [NIST IR 8286 Rev. 1 — Integrating Cybersecurity and Enterprise Risk Management](https://csrc.nist.gov/pubs/ir/8286/r1/final)
- [NIST IR 8286A Rev. 1 — Identifying and Estimating Cybersecurity Risk for Enterprise Risk Management](https://csrc.nist.gov/pubs/ir/8286/a/r1/final)

## 4. Project Deliverables

### Deliverable 1 — Cybersecurity Risk Register

A structured register containing approximately 8–12 risk scenarios.

Each risk will document:

- Risk ID and title.
- Risk scenario.
- Affected business objective, process, or asset.
- Supporting case evidence.
- Inherent likelihood, impact, and risk level.
- Existing controls.
- Residual likelihood, impact, and risk level.
- Recommended treatment.
- Accountable risk owner.
- Assumptions and validation requirements.

### Deliverable 2 — Top-Five Residual-Risk Prioritization

A prioritised view of five residual risks that require leadership attention.

Each selection will include its residual risk level, business significance, selection rationale, and recommended action.

### Deliverable 3 — Executive Risk Summary

A one-page management-facing summary of:

- The overall risk position.
- The most important residual risks.
- Recommended management actions.
- Material assumptions and uncertainties.
- Decisions or escalations requiring leadership attention.

## 6. Evidence and Assessment Principles

- Use the supplied case packet as the primary source of company-specific information.
- Distinguish documented observations from risk scenarios and analyst recommendations.
- Do not assume that an observation automatically represents a separate risk.
- Do not treat proposed safeguards as existing controls.
- Do not invent missing evidence, control effectiveness, or organisational facts.
- Document uncertainty and information that requires validation.
- Explain the reasoning behind risk ratings and treatment decisions.
- Keep the assessment consistent with the supplied risk criteria.
