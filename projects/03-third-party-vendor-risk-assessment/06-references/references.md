## 1. Purpose

This document identifies the sources, frameworks, guidance, and case materials used to complete the fictional third-party vendor risk assessment of CobaltCedar Analytics and its SupportLens SaaS service.

The assessment draws on:

1. NIST cybersecurity and supply-chain risk-management publications.
2. The supplied fictional Avenloft third-party risk review case packet.
3. Vendor evidence described within the case packet.
4. Internal assessment documents and analyst-developed risk analysis.

The references support the assessment methodology, due-diligence approach, evidence review, risk scenarios, remediation recommendations, and proposed vendor decision.

## 2. Primary Frameworks and Standards

### REF-01 — NIST SP 800-30 Rev. 1

**Title:** Guide for Conducting Risk Assessments  
**Publisher:** National Institute of Standards and Technology (NIST)  
**Publication Date:** September 2012  
**Document:** NIST Special Publication 800-30 Rev. 1

**Official publication:**

https://csrc.nist.gov/pubs/sp/800/30/r1/final

**DOI:**

https://doi.org/10.6028/NIST.SP.800-30r1

**Relevance to this project:**

Used as the primary reference for the qualitative risk-assessment process, including:

- Risk assessment preparation and context.
- Threat and vulnerability consideration.
- Likelihood assessment.
- Impact assessment.
- Risk determination.
- Risk response and monitoring considerations.

The assessment applies a qualitative likelihood-and-impact approach. The actual three-level rating matrix used in this project is the matrix specified by the fictional case packet; it is not represented as a universal matrix prescribed by NIST.

### REF-02 — NIST Cybersecurity Framework 2.0

**Title:** The NIST Cybersecurity Framework (CSF) 2.0  
**Publisher:** National Institute of Standards and Technology  
**Publication Date:** February 26, 2024  
**Document:** NIST Cybersecurity White Paper 29

**Official publication:**

https://csrc.nist.gov/pubs/cswp/29/the-nist-cybersecurity-framework-csf-20/final

**DOI:**

https://doi.org/10.6028/NIST.CSWP.29

**Framework resource:**

https://www.nist.gov/cyberframework

**Relevance to this project:**

Provides the overall cybersecurity governance and risk-management framework.

The assessment particularly considers the Govern function and the GV.SC (Cybersecurity Supply Chain Risk Management) category, including governance of supplier relationships, supplier risk requirements, and ongoing supply-chain oversight.

### REF-03 — NIST SP 1305

**Title:** NIST Cybersecurity Framework 2.0: Quick-Start Guide for Cybersecurity Supply Chain Risk Management (C-SCRM)  
**Publisher:** National Institute of Standards and Technology  
**Publication Date:** October 21, 2024  
**Document:** NIST Special Publication 1305

**Official publication:**

https://csrc.nist.gov/pubs/sp/1305/final

**DOI:**

https://doi.org/10.6028/NIST.SP.1305

**Relevance to this project:**

Used to support the practical application of the NIST CSF 2.0 GV.SC category to supplier and service-provider relationships.

Relevant topics include:

- Establishing cybersecurity supply-chain risk-management activities.
- Defining supplier requirements.
- Communicating cybersecurity expectations.
- Integrating supplier considerations into organizational risk management.
- Maintaining supplier oversight.

### REF-04 — NIST SP 1326

**Publisher:** National Institute of Standards and Technology

https://csrc.nist.gov/pubs/sp/1326/final

## 7. Assessment Principles

The assessment follows these documentation principles:

1. Case-specific facts are taken from the supplied fictional case packet.
2. Vendor claims are distinguished from independently supported evidence.
3. Existing controls are distinguished from proposed remediation actions.
4. Analyst judgments are identified as qualitative assessments.
5. Missing evidence is recorded as an evidence gap rather than treated as proof of control failure.
6. No remediation or management approval is assumed without supporting evidence.
7. Current residual risk is kept separate from proposed target residual risk.
8. The project is presented as a fictional training and portfolio exercise.
